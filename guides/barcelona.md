# Barcelona

## Visa and entry
US citizens can visit Spain and the Schengen area visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Your passport should be valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure. Check official EU sources for the current status of the EU Entry/Exit System and ETIAS.

## Getting around
The Metro covers most of the city; a multi-trip T-casual card is cheaper than single tickets. From the airport (BCN), take the Aerobus to Plaça de Catalunya or the L9 Sud metro line.

## Etiquette
Meals run late: lunch around 2 pm and dinner from 9 pm. Barcelona is in Catalonia, and Catalan is spoken alongside Spanish. Watch for pickpockets on La Rambla and in the metro.

## Money
Hotels add a tourist tax per person per night. Sagrada Família and Park Güell use timed tickets that often sell out, so book ahead. Tipping is modest; rounding up is common.

## Neighborhoods and where to stay
The Gothic Quarter has medieval streets and the cathedral. El Born has boutiques and the Picasso Museum. Eixample has Gaudí's modernist buildings. Gràcia feels like a village. Barceloneta is the beach neighborhood.
