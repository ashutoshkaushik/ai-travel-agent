# Rome

## Visa and entry
US citizens can visit Italy and the Schengen area visa-free for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. Your passport should be valid for at least 3 months beyond your planned departure. Check official EU sources for the current status of the EU Entry/Exit System and ETIAS.

## Getting around
The historic center is best explored on foot. The metro has only three lines, so buses and trams fill the gaps; validate paper tickets when you board. The Leonardo Express train runs from Fiumicino Airport (FCO) to Roma Termini in about 32 minutes.

## Etiquette
Cover shoulders and knees to enter churches, including St. Peter's Basilica. Restaurants may add a small "coperto" (cover charge) per person; tipping is not expected but rounding up is appreciated.

## Money
Hotels collect a city tourist tax per person per night, usually paid at check-in or checkout. Book timed tickets for the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums in advance; the Vatican Museums are closed on most Sundays.

## Neighborhoods and where to stay
The Centro Storico has the Pantheon and Piazza Navona. Trastevere has narrow lanes and trattorias. Monti is a village-like area near the Colosseum. Prati is calmer and close to the Vatican.
