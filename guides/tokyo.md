# Tokyo

## Visa and entry
US citizens can visit Japan visa-free for tourism for up to 90 days. Your passport must be valid for the whole stay. Visit Japan Web lets you register immigration and customs details before arrival and get QR codes to speed up the airport process.

## Getting around
Get a Suica or PASMO IC card (or add Suica to your phone's wallet) and tap it on trains, subways, buses, and at convenience stores. The JR Yamanote line loops around the main districts. Most trains stop running around midnight, and taxis are expensive. From Narita (NRT), the Narita Express or Keisei Skyliner reach the city in about an hour.

## Etiquette
Tipping is not practiced and can cause confusion. Keep quiet on trains and put your phone on silent. Queue in marked lines on platforms. Remove your shoes where you see a step up or slippers at an entrance. Eating while walking is frowned upon in many areas.

## Money
Cards are widely accepted, but small shops, shrines, and temples may be cash only. 7-Eleven ATMs accept most foreign cards. Tourists can shop tax-free on purchases over 5,000 yen with a passport.

## Neighborhoods and where to stay
Shinjuku is a transport hub with nightlife. Shibuya has the famous scramble crossing and shopping. Asakusa is traditional, home to Senso-ji. Ginza is upscale shopping. Ueno has museums and a large park.
